Economic & Financial Report: NVIDIA Corp.

Is Nvidia the leader of a Revolution or a Financial Bubble ?

Main objective

This report evaluates whether the high valuation of Nvidia represents a financial bubble. Using public financial data and a data-driven approach, we explore both micro and macro dimensions to find the answer.

The analysis draws on revenue trends, efficiency ratios, balance sheet health, and systemic risk exposure to conclude that Nvidia’s valuation may or may not be supported by fundamentals, placing it at risk of being within a financial bubble.

I. Business Growth

The first section will serve as an introduction to Nvidia’s revenue trend over time and the driving business segments. We begin with certain calculations to gain a general understanding of the subject, and then delve deeper with a more detailed approach.

I.a. Revenue Compound Annual Growth Rate between 2019 & 2025
  • Formula used for this section:
\[CAGR = \left(\frac{Ending\ Value}{Starting\ Value}\right)^\left.\frac{1}{t}\right. - 1 * 100\]

The corporation’s revenue CAGR between 2019 and 2025 is 41.11%. As is, it reveals a very strong growth over a period of seven years. For comparison, the value is nearly 2.5 times that of the Nasdaq-100 and only 10 points lower than Bitcoin’s.

The issue with this metric is that it doesn’t provide further insight. Therefore, a more detailed analysis of Nvidia’s balance sheets is necessary to ascertain whether this CAGR is consistent across the entire period or if it’s only a contributing factor to its high valuation.

  • The following plot (I) accomplishes this by demonstrating how both the overall revenues and those for specific segments have changed annually.

As demonstrated in the attached plot, the significant revenue growth rate originates from the latter part of the period, more precisely in 2024 and 2025, thereby positively influencing its value.

As illustrated in the subplot on the right, there is a clear correlation between total revenue variations and the revenue of its top two segments. <br > In 2023, there is a shift in the relationship between the whole and one unique segment, indicating a heightened concentration risk.

I.b. Segment concentration risk:

While the plot in the previous section indicates which of Nvidia’s segments is the most significant, we will delve deeper by examining quarterly revenue data for each segment. This is also to get a more accurate understanding of the hinted existing concentration risk.

  • Therefore, the next formula was carefully selected to verify the ratio of revenue contribution per segment.
\[Segment\ Contribution\ Ratio = \frac{Revenue\ per\ Segment}{Total\ Revenue}\]

Using SCR’s formula, I generated the next plot on the left, also making trend and pattern recognition more practical.

Plot II-a illustrates that the gaming sector (yellow) has consistently demonstrated financial stability, contributing to Nvidia’s revenue stability. This sector exhibits minimal quarterly fluctuations and constitutes a substantial portion of Nvidia’s overall revenue for the longest period.
Until the second quarter of 2024, the second most significant segment of the company was the data center sector. However, it eventually rose to the top of the list as their GPUs strategically positioned themselves in the rapidly expanding field of artificial intelligence.
In 2025, from plot II-b, we see that 88.3% of their total revenue is dependent on this latest green colored segment, while Gaming now only accounts for 8.7% of it.

That’s why we can affirm that today’s revenue of Nvidia is highly concentrated and dependent on the AI sector’s evolution.

II. Efficiency

This section evaluates how effectively Nvidia converts its revenue into operating profit and identifies where there may be operational pressure or inefficiency. In addition to traditional profitability ratios, we examine cost structure dynamics over time.

II.a. Traditional profitability indicators:
  • The first formula in this subsection will help us measure how efficiently Nvidia converts revenue increases into operating income.
\[Operating\ Leverage\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Δ Operating Income %}}{\text{Δ Revenue %}}\]
  • The next one captures profitability before interest and taxes.
\[Operating\ Margin = \frac{\text{Operating Income}}{\text{Revenue}}\]

Using both of the previous formulas, we get the following table for the studied period.

Though Nvidia’s operating margins mainly increased during our selected timeline, nearly doubling, this is mostly due to increasing total revenue more than decreasing operating expenses.

The operating leverage ratio corroborates this hypothesis, as it has consistently exceeded one for four years out of six, indicating a strong correlation between Nvidia’s profitability and sales. A modest increase in revenue can result in a notable surge in profits. Conversely, a decline in revenue can also have a significant impact on profits, as evidenced in 2020 and 2023. This strategy is advantageous during periods of increased sales; however, it is also risky during times of decreased sales. This is because fixed costs still must be accounted for regardless of sales volume.

In other words, the higher leverage indicates that the company has significant fixed costs, resulting in high operating expenses. Consequently, the break-even point for sales is dependent on covering these costs. This, among other factors, contributed to the company’s emergence as a dominant player in the AI GPU data center. They possessed the necessary assets and strategic timing to capitalize on this market opportunity.

II.b. Cost structure dynamics over time:

Moving forward, we will be examining the various components of the company’s revenue to gain a more comprehensive understanding of its internal efficiency.

  • For all the reasons outlined in the prior sections, I selected two periods for comparison in this plot which also align with the concept of revenue concentration risk in one segment.

An analysis of the revenue structure of the two periods indicates a substantial increase in net income, from an average of 27.2% between 2019 and 2023, to 45.0% in the 2024-2025 period. This reflects a significant increase of 17.8 percentage points between the two periods.

This is attributable to the enhancement of the internal cost components of revenue, despite the deterioration of the external components (taxes and interest). COGS decreased from 38.7% to 30.0%, reflecting an 8.7-point reduction. Similarly, OpEx declined from 34.0% to 20.9%, indicating a 13.1-point drop. However, Taxes and Interests increased from less than 0.1% to 4.2%, showing a 4.1-point rise. The resulting figures are as follows: 17.8% is equivalent to 8.7% plus 13.1% minus 4.1%, with a 0.1% margin of error.

While these results may be considered encouraging, it is important to note that they are based on percentages and, as such, require further analysis to ensure a comprehensive interpretation. When we consider the revenue growth and compare it to the improvement of the cost structure for the same periods, the latter loses some of its appeal. However, we can affirm that Nvidia is more efficient now than in the past.

III. Alignments between intrinsic & external values:

This section presents a comparison between internal value indicators and external ones to understand the value’s current relationship between these aspects.

III.a. Fundamentals against Valuation:
  • Enterprise Value (EV) is a more insightful indicator than Market Capitalization (MC) alone. It is selected as an in-between internal and external indicator because it also takes into account the capital structure of the company. Here’s its formula:
\[Enterprise\ Value\ = \text{average Market Cap} + \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash and Cash Equivalents}\]

As illustrated in the above table, prior to the surge in the company’s data center AI segment (2023 and beyond), enterprise value and market capitalization exhibited a period of significant growth over a three-year span, while net income demonstrated a comparatively slower progression.
This could serve as an indicator of a potential market bubble, as it shows that investor expectations have become uncoupled from reality. This suggests the possible presence of investor exuberance at the time.

However, in 2024, the situation changed, as EV and MC growth percentages were three times lower than the net income’s, suggesting potential undervaluation at the time.

As the data for the company’s Total Debt & Cash and Cash Equivalents for 2025 has not yet been made available, it has not been possible to estimate Enterprise Value (EV) and thereby make a comparison that would allow us to determine whether the 2024 trend is merely transient or if it will continue into 2025. A thorough review of past data indicates that the corporation’s capital management strategy involves a degree of risk, yet it has produced favorable outcomes.

III.b. Capital Structure & Risk profile
  • Next, we will calculate the debt-to-equity ratio to better understand how the company finances its assets. This will allow us to more clearly evaluate the risks in their capital structure and explain why their taxes and interest rates increased so much between our two previously selected periods: 2019-2023 and 2024-2025.
\[Debt\ to\ Equity\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Shareholder Equity}}\]
  • This is incomplete because it doesn’t address the company’s ability to pay off its debt. That’s why we use both Earnings Before Interests, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) and Net Debt to evaluate it.
\[Net\ Debt\ to\ EBITDA\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Net Debt}}{\text{EBITDA}}\]

For the first period, in 2023 and prior years, the debt-to-equity ratio nearly reached 0.5. This means that for every dollar of equity the company had, it owed 50 cents to creditors, representing half of the shareholders’ equity in debt. At the same time, the second ratio also increased, indicating an ability to cover debt that is deteriorating, though not to the point of being problematic, as it remained below 2.

At that time, the enterprise’s condition was made somewhat fragile by this significant leverage. This is the reason for the increased taxes and interest rates, although this was not an unusual event in the tech industry. The situation has since improved significantly, as evidenced by an 18-fold decrease in the ratio.

Interestingly, the average net debt-to-EBITDA ratio from 2019 to 2025 is 0.115, which implies low credit risk. While the ratio exceeds 1 in 2021 and 2023, this is largely offset by negative ratios in 2020 and 2025. On average, the company has more than enough cash to pay off its debt while generating a surplus, which explains the lower credit risk.

III.c. Ownership structure:
  • In this final subdivision, we examine the ownership structure of market shares held by insiders and outsiders to identify potential control and exit risks.

As shown in the chart above, the possible impact of exit risk on the stock decreased from 2019 to 2025 as the number of shares held by insiders and the top three institutions decreased because these groups were selling.

Their lower ownership percentage over the years may indicate that they perceive the current market valuation as unsustainable and/or risky, requiring a more cautious and conservative approach. However, their percentage of ownership is still higher than it was at the beginning of the period.

IV. Macro Positioning

This part of our analysis will add a macro/market structure perspective, which is essential, especially when a company pivots into a capital-intensive and hype-sensitive sector, such as AI infrastructure. When a segment like AI-focused data centers becomes a company’s dominant revenue stream and key justification for its valuation, we must assess not only how well the company is performing but also how durable and defensible its performance is in a competitive, macroeconomic context.

Note: Nvidia publishes its data in the first month of each year, while other companies, such as Intel and AMD, publish theirs at the end of the previous year.
For example, “2025” could refer to the end of 2024 or the beginning of January 2025.

IV.b. Competitive landscape:

Now, we will focus on companies involved in the global AI industry, even though not all of them are direct competitors of our subject. Our objective here is simply to determine Nvidia’s importance in this field, where it generates most of its revenue.

We will use three indicators to achieve that.

  • The first is: \(Market\ Share\ = \frac{\text{Individual Revenue}}{\text{Total Sector Revenue}}\)

  • Then: \(Revenue\ CAGR = \left(\frac{\text{Revenue 2025}}{\text{Revenue 2023}}\right)^\frac{1}{3} - 1 * 100\)

  • And, lastly: \(Price\ to\ Sales\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Market Value per Share}}{\text{Sales per Share}} \text{with: Sales per Share} = \frac{\text{Sales total Revenue}}{\text{Outstanding Shares}}\)

As demonstrated in our table, Nvidia not only holds the largest market share in the sector, but also exhibits the strongest revenue growth for the selected period, even when indirect competitors are included. This suggests that, despite perceptions of AI as a financial bubble driven by excessive investor optimism, Nvidia stands out as a relatively secure investment due to its strategic position. This, in turn, partially explains the high Price to Sales ratio (P/S), as well as the cautious stance adopted by insiders and leading institutional investors.

However, this part of our case study would be incomplete without comparing our company to its direct competitors, as they have the most potential to threaten its position.

Focusing on the GPU AI branch, Nvidia’s market share increases by a factor of 2.5, putting it ahead of its competitors, such as Intel and AMD, in the AI data center chip segment.

  • While hyperscalers like AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, and Oracle build massive AI infrastructure and develop some custom chips, Nvidia GPUs remain the center of AI compute in most data centers
  • Although IBM, Dell, and Cisco are important players in hybrid AI and data center infrastructure, they do not directly challenge Nvidia’s dominance in AI chip hardware.
  • NVIDIA’s vertically integrated platform and software ecosystem, including CUDA and AI frameworks, further consolidates its leadership position and creates barriers for competitors.

In conclusion, Nvidia’s status as the dominant market leader in the AI data center sector, especially in AI GPUs, is marked by a significant market share that eclipses competitors like Intel and AMD. The company’s role in cloud providers’ AI infrastructure is critical, with its performance surpassing that of other major players in this segment.
