# **Economic & Financial Report: NVIDIA Corp.**  
### *Is Nvidia the leader of a Revolution or a Financial Bubble ?*

## **Main objective**

This report evaluates whether the high valuation of Nvidia represents a financial bubble.
Using public financial data and a data-driven approach, we explore both **micro** and **macro** dimensions to find the answer.  

The analysis draws on revenue trends, efficiency ratios, balance sheet health, and systemic risk exposure to conclude that **Nvidia’s valuation may or may not be supported by fundamentals**, placing it at **risk of being within a financial bubble**.

##### **I.a. Revenue Compound Annual Growth Rate between 2019 & 2025**
  - Formula used for this section:


$$
CAGR = \left(\frac{Ending\ Value}{Starting\ Value}\right)^\left.\frac{1}{t}\right. - 1 * 100
$$

The corporation's revenue CAGR between 2019 and 2025 is **41.11%**. As is, it reveals a very strong growth over a period of seven years. For comparison, the value is nearly _2.5 times that of the Nasdaq-100_ and only _10 points lower than Bitcoin's_.


The issue with this metric is that it doesn't provide further insight. Therefore, a more detailed analysis of Nvidia's balance sheets is necessary to ascertain whether this CAGR is consistent across the entire period or if it's only a contributing factor to its high valuation.
  

  - The following plot (**I**) accomplishes this by demonstrating how both the overall revenues and those for specific segments have changed annually.

### **II. Efficiency**
This section evaluates how effectively Nvidia converts its revenue into operating profit and identifies where there may be operational pressure or inefficiency. In addition to traditional profitability ratios, we examine cost structure dynamics over time.


##### **II.a. Traditional profitability indicators:**
  - The first formula in this subsection will help us measure how efficiently Nvidia converts revenue increases into operating income.


$$
Operating\ Leverage\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Δ Operating Income %}}{\text{Δ Revenue %}} 
$$


  - The next one captures profitability before interest and taxes.


$$
Operating\ Margin = \frac{\text{Operating Income}}{\text{Revenue}} 
$$


Using both of the previous formulas, we get the following table for the studied period.

    <div class="col-sm mt-3 mt-md-0">
        {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/nvidia_table_1_efficiency.png" title="table 1: Profitability" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
    </div>
</div>

Though Nvidia's operating margins mainly increased during our selected timeline, nearly doubling, this is mostly due to increasing total revenue more than decreasing operating expenses.


The operating leverage ratio corroborates this hypothesis, as it has consistently exceeded one for four years out of six, indicating a strong correlation between Nvidia's profitability and sales. A modest increase in revenue can result in a notable surge in profits. Conversely, a decline in revenue can also have a significant impact on profits, as evidenced in 2020 and 2023. This strategy is advantageous during periods of increased sales; however, it is also risky during times of decreased sales. This is because fixed costs still must be accounted for regardless of sales volume.


In other words, the higher leverage indicates that the company has significant fixed costs, resulting in high operating expenses. Consequently, the break-even point for sales is dependent on covering these costs. This, among other factors, contributed to the company's emergence as a dominant player in the AI GPU data center. They possessed the necessary assets and strategic timing to capitalize on this market opportunity.

Moving forward, we will be examining the various components of the company's revenue to gain a more comprehensive understanding of its internal efficiency.


  - For all the reasons outlined in the prior sections, I selected two periods for comparison in this plot which also align with the concept of revenue concentration risk in one segment.

An analysis of the revenue structure of the two periods indicates a substantial increase in net income, from an average of 27.2% between 2019 and 2023, to 45.0% in the 2024-2025 period. This reflects a significant increase of 17.8 percentage points between the two periods. <br />


This is attributable to the enhancement of the internal cost components of revenue, despite the deterioration of the external components (taxes and interest). COGS decreased from 38.7% to 30.0%, reflecting an 8.7-point reduction. Similarly, OpEx declined from 34.0% to 20.9%, indicating a 13.1-point drop. However, Taxes and Interests increased from less than 0.1% to 4.2%, showing a 4.1-point rise. The resulting figures are as follows: 17.8% is equivalent to 8.7% plus 13.1% minus 4.1%, with a 0.1% margin of error.


While these results may be considered encouraging, it is important to note that they are based on percentages and, as such, require further analysis to ensure a comprehensive interpretation. When we consider the revenue growth and compare it to the improvement of the cost structure for the same periods, the latter loses some of its appeal. However, we can affirm that _Nvidia is more efficient now than in the past._




### **III. Alignments between intrinsic & external values:**  
This section presents a comparison between internal value indicators and external ones to understand the value's current relationship between these aspects.


##### **III.a. Fundamentals against Valuation:**
  - Enterprise Value (EV) is a more insightful indicator than Market Capitalization (MC) alone. It is selected as an in-between internal and external indicator because it also takes into account the capital structure of the company. Here's its formula:


$$
Enterprise\ Value\ = \text{average Market Cap} + \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash and Cash Equivalents}
$$

As illustrated in the above table, prior to the surge in the company's data center AI segment (2023 and beyond), enterprise value and market capitalization exhibited a period of significant growth over a three-year span, while net income demonstrated a comparatively slower progression. <br />
This could serve as an indicator of a potential market bubble, as it shows that investor expectations have become uncoupled from reality. This suggests the possible presence of investor exuberance at the time. <br />


However, in 2024, the situation changed, as EV and MC growth percentages were three times lower than the net income's, suggesting potential undervaluation at the time.

A thorough review of past data indicates that the corporation's capital management strategy involves a degree of risk, yet it has produced favorable outcomes.


##### **III.b. Capital Structure & Risk profile**  
  - Next, we will calculate the debt-to-equity ratio to better understand how the company finances its assets. This will allow us to more clearly evaluate the risks in their capital structure and explain why their taxes and interest rates increased so much between our two previously selected periods: 2019-2023 and 2024-2025.


$$
Debt\ to\ Equity\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Shareholder Equity}} 
$$

  - This is incomplete because it doesn't address the company's ability to pay off its debt. That's why we use both Earnings Before Interests, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) and Net Debt to evaluate it.


$$
Net\ Debt\ to\ EBITDA\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Net Debt}}{\text{EBITDA}} 
$$

For the first period, in 2023 and prior years, the debt-to-equity ratio nearly reached 0.5. This means that for every dollar of equity the company had, it owed 50 cents to creditors, representing half of the shareholders' equity in debt. At the same time, the second ratio also increased, indicating an ability to cover debt that is deteriorating, though not to the point of being problematic, as it remained below 2. <br />


At that time, the enterprise's condition was made somewhat fragile by this significant leverage. This is the reason for the increased taxes and interest rates, although this was not an unusual event in the tech industry. The situation has since improved significantly, as evidenced by an 18-fold decrease in the ratio.


Interestingly, the average net debt-to-EBITDA ratio from 2019 to 2025 is 0.115, which implies low credit risk. While the ratio exceeds 1 in 2021 and 2023, this is largely offset by negative ratios in 2020 and 2025. On average, the company has more than enough cash to pay off its debt while generating a surplus, which explains the lower credit risk.

##### **IV.b. Competitive landscape:**
Now, we will focus on companies involved in the global AI industry, even though not all of them are direct competitors of our subject. Our objective here is simply to determine Nvidia's importance in this field, where it generates most of its revenue.

We will use three indicators to achieve that.


  - The first is:
$$
Market\ Share\ = \frac{\text{Individual Revenue}}{\text{Total Sector Revenue}}
$$


 - Then: 
$$
Revenue\ CAGR = \left(\frac{\text{Revenue 2025}}{\text{Revenue 2023}}\right)^\frac{1}{3} - 1 * 100
$$


  - And, lastly: 
$$
Price\ to\ Sales\ Ratio = \frac{\text{Market Value per Share}}{\text{Sales per Share}} \text{with: Sales per Share} = \frac{\text{Sales total Revenue}}{\text{Outstanding Shares}}
$$

As we can see in our table, Nvidia not only has the biggest market share in the sector, but also the best revenue growth for our selected period — even when indirect competitors are included. This shows that, although AI may be considered a financial bubble due to excessive investor optimism for the technology, Nvidia is one of the safest investments because of its position. However, this part of our case study would be incomplete without comparing our company to its direct competitors, as they have the most potential to threaten its position.


Focusing on the GPU AI branch, Nvidia’s market share increases by a factor of 2.5, putting it ahead of its competitors, such as Intel and AMD, in the AI data center chip segment.
  - While hyperscalers like AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, and Oracle build massive AI infrastructure and develop some custom chips, Nvidia GPUs remain the center of AI compute in most data centers
  - Although IBM, Dell, and Cisco are important players in hybrid AI and data center infrastructure, they do not directly challenge Nvidia’s dominance in AI chip hardware.
  - NVIDIA’s vertically integrated platform and software ecosystem, including CUDA and AI frameworks, further consolidates its leadership position and creates barriers for competitors.


In conclusion, Nvidia's status as the dominant market leader in the AI data center sector, especially in AI GPUs, is marked by a significant market share that eclipses competitors like Intel and AMD. The company's role in cloud providers' AI infrastructure is critical, with its performance surpassing that of other major players in this segment.
